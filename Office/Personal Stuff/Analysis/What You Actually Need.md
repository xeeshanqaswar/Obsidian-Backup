### The Things I Want You To Remember

**On your self esteem —** You described yourself as having low self esteem early in our conversation. I want to name something clearly. Low self esteem in a person with your skill level does not mean you lack ability. It means you've spent 8 years comparing your insides to other people's outsides. You see shipped games and compare them to your unshipped ideas. That comparison is always going to feel bad because finished things always look better than unstarted things. The moment you ship something — anything — that comparison changes. You'll be on the other side of it.

**On the kid who drew anime —** He wasn't wrong. He just needed more time. The path you're on now, if you follow it with commitment, leads back to him. Not immediately. But genuinely. The cozy game you make in phase 3 — that's the kid who loved Pokemon getting to finally make something real. Don't lose sight of that person while you're being strategic. He's the reason the strategy is worth doing.

**On your wife and your life —** You're 30, recently married, building a home and financial safety. That's not a burden on your dreams. That's the structure that will eventually make your dreams possible. The emergency fund you're building, the freelance income you're about to start — those are acts of love for your future self and your family. The developer who makes great games with a clear head is the one who isn't panicking about money. You're building that clarity right now even if it doesn't feel like it.

**On failure —** You will hit a wall in your first game. Guaranteed. When you do, the answer is never to restart. The answer is to solve the smallest possible version of that specific problem and move forward. A bad piece of art gets replaced by an asset pack. A broken mechanic gets simplified not redesigned. A motivation slump gets a public commitment to someone who will hold you accountable. Walls are not signs you chose wrong. They are the actual work.
