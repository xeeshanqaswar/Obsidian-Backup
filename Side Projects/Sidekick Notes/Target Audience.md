### What UX / competitive researchers actually do

The relevant slice of their work (the browser-based part) is essentially structured observation. A few concrete jobs:

A **competitive teardown** — they go through 3–8 competitor sites/products and systematically document how each one handles something: onboarding, pricing page, checkout flow, empty states, messaging. For each competitor they capture screenshots, jot what's good/bad, and tag which pattern it is. At the end they assemble it into a deliverable — a slide deck, a Figma board, a Notion doc, or a PDF report for a client or their PM.

A **UX audit / heuristic evaluation** — reviewing one live or staging site against a checklist (often Nielsen's 10 heuristics), noting each issue anchored to the specific screen and element where it occurs, rating severity, and handing a prioritized list to a design/dev team.

A **design critique or content/SEO competitor review** — similar shape: move across many pages, capture the visual, write the observation, keep it traceable to its source.

The through-line, and this is the important part: they capture **visual + text observations across many pages, need each observation anchored to its source, organize by project or competitor, and then export the whole thing into a deliverable.** Their pain today is that screenshots end up in a folder with no context about which page or why, notes live somewhere separate, and reassembling it all at the end is the worst part of the job.

That "capture-anchored-to-source, organize by project, produce a deliverable" loop is exactly where Sidekick's existing pieces (floating notes pinned to a page, screenshots attached to notes, color-coded collections) already fit — and it tells you cleanly where free ends and paid begins.
