# **Decorations are even cheaper**

One aquarium could have:

10 rocks  
10 plants  
10 corals  
10 props.

That’s:

40 objects.

But players can place them differently.

So thousands of players can have visually different aquariums from the same 40 objects.

# **Aquarium can exploit this even more aggressively**

Fish are easier than humanoid characters.

Imagine one base fish mesh has three material zones:

**Body**

**Fins**

**Pattern**

Then define colors/materials from data.

Fish #01:

orange body  
white fins  
black pattern

Fish #02:

blue body  
yellow fins  
white pattern

Fish #03:

black body  
red fins  
gold pattern

Same:

mesh  
rig  
animations  
AI/swimming behavior.

Different material configuration.

Already looks like three collectibles.

---

# **Then rarity adds another layer**

Suppose every species can have:

**Common**

Normal material.

**Rare**

Different coloration.

**Epic**

Special pattern + subtle particles.

**Legendary**

Unique material + glow + particles + maybe crown/visual attachment.

Now:

25 fish models × 4 rarity appearances

=

### **100 collectibles.**

But you’ve created only:

**25 actual models.**

That’s the kind of production multiplication we want.

# **There’s another trick: size**

This is particularly useful with animals/fish.

Suppose one shark model exists.

We can have:

Baby Reef Shark

↓

Reef Shark

↓

Alpha Reef Shark

with:

scale

texture

stats

particles

changing as it evolves.

One asset becomes an entire progression line.

For example:

**Level 1**

Tiny fish.

↓

**Level 10**

Medium fish.

↓

**Level 25**

Large fish + stronger colors.

↓

**Level 50**

Large + special pattern.

↓

**Ascended**

Glow + VFX.

The player watched their fish **grow**.

	That’s actually more emotionally valuable than simply unlocking another model.
